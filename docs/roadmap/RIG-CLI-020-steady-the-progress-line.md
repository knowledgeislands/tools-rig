## Goal

Somebody watching `rig apply`, `rig status`, or `rig doctor` sees a progress bar that stays in one place, in a line whose columns are where they were on the frame before, and that uses the width their terminal actually has.

## Context

The transient bar moves in two ways while a phase runs, and neither is about progress.

The phase name is printed through `%-22s`, which pads a short name but does not truncate a long one. `config validation` fits; the `configuration validation` it replaced was twenty-four characters and `configuration discovery` twenty-three, so the bar's opening bracket sat one or two columns further right for the whole of the configuration phases than it did for every other phase.

Worse, the completed count was printed after the bar rather than before it. `9/133` becoming `10/133` and then `100/133` shifts every character to its right by one column and then by one more, so the item identity beside it slid twice during a run of any size, and the reader's eye had to re-find it each time.

The bar itself was sixteen characters regardless of the terminal. On a hundred-and-twenty-column window that spends an eighth of the line on the one field that carries the progress, and leaves the rest to an item detail that was already being elided.

The item text spent its width poorly too. `onedrive via homebrew` reads as prose and buries the provider at the end; `homebrew:onedrive` is four characters shorter, sorts and scans by provider, and matches how the rest of Rig writes a qualified identity. `succeeded`, `skipped`, and `failed` cost nine, seven, and six columns to say what `ok`, `skip`, and `fail` say in four.

`ki repo audit` and `ki repo conform` already solved this shape: fixed fields either side, an elastic bar in the middle, the whole line padded to the terminal's width so a redraw erases its predecessor without remembering how long it was.

## Boundary

This is the transient bar's geometry and the wording of the identities and results that Rig writes into it. It is not a change to which phases report, to what counts as an item, to when a count advances, to the outcome line, or to progress's stderr-only contract.

The `RIG_PROGRESS=lines` renderer keeps its full words, its `[scope]` suffix, and its one-event-per-line shape: whole lines do not need geometry. It picks up the shortened phase names and the provider-qualified identities because those come from the call sites both renderers share.

## Current state

`rig_progress_bar_render` in `src/rig/00-runtime.bash` composes `'\rrig: %-22s [%s] %s/%s'` and then appends the summary, tracking the previous frame's length in `RIG_PROGRESS_RENDERED` so it can pad the difference away. `rig_progress_make_bar` fills a fixed `RIG_PROGRESS_BAR_WIDTH=16` with `#` and `-` and has no notion of an item in flight. `rig_progress_columns` already reads `COLUMNS` and then `stty size <&2`, so the terminal width is available and is spent only on eliding the item text.

Thirty-one call sites across `20-orchestration.bash` and `40-publication-lifecycle.bash` build an item as `"$thing via $provider"`, and four phases in `10-configuration.bash` are named `configuration <something>`.

## Steps

- [x] Give every field but the bar a fixed width, and put the completed count between the phase name and the bar so a digit's growth cannot move anything to its right.
- [x] Derive the bar's width from the terminal, bounded above so a wide window spends its surplus on the item identity instead, and degrading through the identity and then the phase name before giving the bar up entirely on a very narrow one.
- [x] Adopt the three-zone bar `ki repo audit` uses — `#` complete, `>` in flight, `.` pending — so the two tools look like one house.
- [x] Pad each frame to the terminal's width so a redraw erases its predecessor without tracking the previous frame's length, and retire `RIG_PROGRESS_RENDERED`.
- [x] Rewrite the item identities as `provider:thing`, and shorten the four `configuration <something>` phase names to `config <something>`.
- [x] Shorten the bar's result words to `ok`, `skip`, and `fail`, and its phase summary to `ok=N skip=N fail=N`, leaving the `lines` renderer's full words untouched.

## Discussion

### Why the count moved left

Putting the completed count after the bar reads naturally — bar, then how far along — but it is the one field that changes width during a phase, and everything after a variable-width field inherits its jitter. Ahead of the bar it jitters nothing, because the bar that follows it is the field whose width is already being computed.

### What a wide terminal should buy

A bar longer than about fifty characters conveys nothing a shorter one does not; the eye reads the proportion, not the character count. So surplus width above that bound goes to the item identity, which can always use it, and which was the field being elided in the first place.
